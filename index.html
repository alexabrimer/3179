The Race for the Brownlow

Every season, AFL players compete for the most prestigious individual award. Explore who gets the most votes, what team they come from, and what players in the lead actually have in common.

  • 812players eligible
  • 18clubs
  • 1,242Brownlow votes
  • 47votes for Nick Daicos

The Brownlow Medal rewards the “fairest and best” player of the home-and-away season. After every game umpires award votes to the three best players on the ground, and the votes are added up at season’s end. Hover over the charts below for details, and use the dropdowns to explore specifics.

Section 1

The pipeline

AFL players come from all across Australia, but where are the most players from? Which State is recruited from the most and how are players traded?

Chart 1

Victoria is still the heartland

Every AFL player starts somewhere. Of the 786 players whose recruiting state we know, 430 (55%) came from Victoria. Western Australia (124) and South Australia (111) come next, while Tasmania, the Northern Territory and the ACT together supply 31.

Choose a club from the dropdown to see where it finds its players. Adelaide, Fremantle and West Coast draw most heavily from their home state, but even Brisbane and Sydney recruit more from Victoria than from their own: 16 of Brisbane’s 41 known players, and 20 of Sydney’s 44.

Home-state recruiting is strong in the west and south, but Victoria supplies talent to every club.

Chart 2

Clubs also recruit players through trading

Players don’t always stay where they start. This map shows the trades from the 2025 trade period, which reshaped lists in the months before the 2026 season.

Choose a club from the dropdown to focus on the players it sent away and brought in. Lines connect the clubs involved in each trade.

Section 2

Individual Glory

Which clubs, and which kinds of player collected the Brownlow votes in 2026?

Chart 3

Sydney and Fremantle rise to the top

With 1,242 votes awarded across the season, Sydney’s players polled the most at 102, narrowly ahead of Fremantle with 100. At the other extreme, Richmond’s players weren’t impressing the umpires with just 15 votes given.

Nine clubs are based in Melbourne, so use the dropdown to zoom in to Victoria, Melbourne and other states to see the clubs more closely.

Circle area shows the total votes polled by each club’s players.

Chart 4

Midfielders collect the votes

Of the 1,242 votes, 764 (about 62%) went to midfielders. The biggest single cell belongs to midfielders aged 25 or 26, who polled 216 votes between them.

Ruckmen are the exception. Of their 77 votes, 44 went to players aged 31 or over.

46% of all votes went to players aged 23 to 26.

Chart 5

How old are AFL players?

The average player in 2026 is 24, but the average player who polled Brownlow votes is 26.6. It would seem that experience is a factor in the decision of the umpires.

West Coast’s players are the youngest with a median age 22.6 and received just 31 Brownlow votes, while Brisbane has the oldest team with a median age of 25.8 and polled 86. However this does not apply across the board as Fremantle has a median age of 24.0 and still polled 100 votes in the 2026 season.

The oldest player is Collingwood’s Scott Pendlebury, at 38. The youngest is Hawthorn’s Aidan Schubert, at 18.

Section 3

The contenders

Now let’s look at the ten players who polled the most votes: Nick Daicos (47), Bailey Smith (36), Marcus Bontempelli (34), Max Gawn (28), Will Ashcroft, Patrick Cripps and Harry Sheezel (27 each), Isaac Heeney and Zak Butters (26 each) and Izak Rankine (25).

Chart 6

How the top ten earned their votes

Each square is one Brownlow vote. After every game the umpires give 3, 2 and 1 votes to the three best players, so a 3-vote game adds three dark squares to a player’s row, a 2-vote game adds two mid-blue squares and a 1-vote game adds one light square. Hover over a square to see which round it came from.

Nick Daicos earned 39 of his 47 votes from 13 games where he was judged the best player on the ground. Izak Rankine polled in only 10 games, the fewest of the ten, but six were worth three votes. Zak Butters polled in 13 games, yet only four were 3-vote games.

Daicos polled the maximum three votes in 13 games. The next best is Bailey Smith with nine.

Chart 7

The Disposal Tally

This chart follows the ten players round by round, ranking them by their running total of disposals.

Nick Daicos finished the home-and-away rounds on top with 767, ahead of Bailey Smith (718) and Harry Sheezel (669). Butters is the one to watch: he played only 17 games, yet averaged almost 30 disposals in each.

Daicos averaged 34.9 disposals a game over the home-and-away season.

Chart 8

Moving the ball

Disposal efficiency is the share of a player’s kicks and handballs that help the team. Each shape shows how a player’s efficiency varied from game to game: the wider the shape, the more often he landed there.

Marcus Bontempelli and Harry Sheezel had the highest median game, at 75%, against 66% for Bailey Smith.

Chart 9

Same prize, different strengths

The ten contenders don’t play the same way. Each line is one player, and the higher it sits on an axis, the better he rates among the ten. Click a name in the legend to follow one player across the page.

Daicos leads for disposals, Sheezel for marks and pressure, Heeney for goals, Cripps for tackles and clearances, and Bailey Smith for inside 50s and metres gained. Ruckman Max Gawn sits at the bottom for disposals, tackles, pressure and metres, yet still polled 28 votes.

Chart 10

What does a vote-winning game look like?

What separates a vote-winning game from a quiet one? This chart compares the average statistics in games where a player polled votes with games where he polled none. A value of 1× means no difference, and 2× means twice as many.

In 3-vote games players averaged about four times as many clearances (5.6 against 1.4) and 3.6 times as many goals (1.8 against 0.5). Tackles (1.6×) and marks (1.3×) separate vote-winning games far less. Use the dropdown to compare 2-vote and 1-vote games.

Clearances and goals stand out the most; tackles and marks stand out the least.

Charts 11 & 12

Head to head: how do two contenders compare?

We start with Daicos and Smith, who finished first and second in the votes, but you can pick any two of the ten. Over the whole season, finals included, Daicos had more disposals (797 to 774) and kicked more goals (26 to 11).

Smith did more of the hard work without the ball: he laid 117 tackles to Daicos’s 58, about twice as many. The radar shows each player’s overall shape compared with the other contenders, and the bars compare totals statistic by statistic.

Conclusion

What the data says

1

Votes follow midfielders in their mid-20s

Midfielders took about 62% of the votes, and players aged 23 to 26 took 46%. In a 3-vote game a player wins about four times as many clearances as in a game with no votes.

2

Stars win in different ways

Daicos wins with volume, Smith with tackling and run, and Cripps with tackles and clearances. Gawn polled 28 votes while sitting at the bottom for disposals and tackles.

3

Victoria feeds the whole competition

55% of players with a known recruiting state came from Victoria. Even Brisbane and Sydney recruit more from Victoria than from their own state.

Data and Credits

Data sources

  • Player details (club, position, birth date, recruited from): AFL.com, 2026.
  • Brownlow votes: AFL Tables, 2026 Brownlow Medal (afltables.com).
  • Match statistics (disposals, clearances, goals, tackles and other per-game numbers): AFL Tables, 2026 player statistics (afltables.com).
  • Trades: DraftGuru, 2025 trade period (draftguru.com.au).

Method notes

  • Votes (AFL Tables) were joined to player details (AFL) by club, surname and first initial. All 183 players who polled votes were matched.
  • Age is measured on 1 July 2026, the middle of the season.
  • The recruiting state comes from each player’s recruiting history. It is unknown for 26 of 812 players, who are left out of the first map.
